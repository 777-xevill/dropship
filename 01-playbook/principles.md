# Principles

Hard rules I don't re-litigate. Each one should have cost me something to learn.

**Format:** rule, then the evidence. A rule without evidence is a vibe, and vibes get deleted at review.

---

## Standing rules

### 1. Creative volume beats creative polish
The ad wins on hook and pacing, not render quality. A mediocre clip with a sharp first 3 seconds outperforms a beautiful render with a boring open.
> Evidence: _(fill after first test round)_

### 2. Never test a product without unit economics written down first
If I can't state break-even ROAS before launch, I'm gambling, not testing.
> Evidence: _(fill)_

### 3. Kill decisions are made against a pre-committed number, not a feeling
Decide the kill threshold before spending. Written down. See `unit-economics.md`.
> Evidence: _(fill)_

### 4. AI creative is for testing angles cheaply. Winners get upgraded.
Broad pattern in the market: use AI to test many angles at low cost, then put real production (or a real creator) behind whatever wins. Consumer research is not flattering to purely AI ads — people tend to rate them as more annoying and boring than human-made ones. So AI is a discovery tool, not the final answer for a scaling product.
> Evidence: _(validate in my own account)_

### 5. Vendor-reported numbers are marketing
Tool sites claim things like 4x CTR lifts and ROAS jumping from 2.3x to 6.1x. None of it is audited. My only trustworthy numbers are in `03-logs/`.
> Evidence: standing rule.
